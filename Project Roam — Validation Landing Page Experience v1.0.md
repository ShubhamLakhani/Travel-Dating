# Project Roam — Validation Landing Page Experience

**Stage:** Pre-MVP Validation  
**Goal:** Test whether travelers will submit a real upcoming trip to discover potential romantic matches.  
**Primary conversion:** Real trip submitted  
**Secondary conversion:** Early-access signup  
**Working brand:** Project Roam

---

# 1. Core Principle

The website should **feel like the beginning of the product**, not like a startup marketing page.

We do NOT want:

Hero → lots of text → features → testimonials → email field.

We want:

**Destination → Dates → Match Preview → Value → Early Access**

The visitor should experience the central idea within the first few seconds.

---

# 12. Safety Section

This should be one of the strongest sections.

Headline:

# Travel dating should feel safer.

Supporting copy:

**Meeting someone new is different when you're far from home. Roam is being designed with travel-specific safety built into the experience.**

Four cards:

### Verified profiles

Selfie verification designed to reduce fake profiles.

### Approximate location

Your hotel, hostel and exact live location are never shown publicly.

### Share your date

Privately share who you're meeting, where and when with someone you trust.

### Check-ins

Set a time for Roam to ask whether your date is going okay.

Small line underneath:

**Safety and verification features will never require a premium subscription.**

That is a very strong brand signal.

# 22. FAQ Section

Keep this short.

### Is Project Roam live?

**We're currently validating and building the first version. Early-access members will be invited as matching opens in selected destinations.**

### Are the profiles shown here real?

**No. Profiles shown in the preview are examples designed to demonstrate how Project Roam will work.**

This transparency is essential.

### Is Project Roam only for solo travelers?

**Solo travelers are our initial focus, but travelers visiting with friends may also join.**

### Can I meet locals?

**Yes. You'll be able to choose whether you'd like to meet travelers, locals or both.**

### Will my exact location be visible?

**No. Project Roam is being designed to avoid exposing exact accommodation or live location publicly.**

### Is Project Roam 18+?

**Yes. Project Roam is intended only for adults aged 18 and over.**

# 24. Visual Direction

The site should feel:

**Travel × Dating × Premium Social**

Not:

Travel agency.

Corporate SaaS.

Cheap dating website.

Backpacking blog.

Suggested visual mood:

Large typography.

Generous spacing.

Destination photography used selectively.

Rounded product cards.

Soft motion.

Beautiful destination search.

Passport/trip-inspired elements.

Modern mobile-product aesthetic.

Map elements only when meaningful.

Profile cards should feel premium rather than Tinder clones.

# 26. Motion

Use subtle interaction.

Good:

Destination card transitions.

Trip-date animation.

Profile cards appearing after search.

Overlap badge animation.

Gentle travel-route line.

Form-step transitions.

Bad:

Huge parallax sections.

Excessive scroll effects.

Constant movement.

Anything that makes the page feel like an agency portfolio.

The product interaction itself should provide the interest.

# 29. Primary Metrics

Our dashboard should emphasize five numbers.

## 1. Trip Search Rate

`destination searches / landing visitors`

Are people interested enough to engage?

## 2. Match Intent Rate

`Find Travel Matches clicks / landing visitors`

Do people want the proposed outcome?

## 3. Trip Submission Rate

`real trips submitted / landing visitors`

One of our strongest validation metrics.

## 4. Early Access Conversion

`completed signups / landing visitors`

Does interest survive the signup request?

## 5. Match Density

How many potentially compatible users overlap by:

**destination + dates + preferences**

This is potentially the most important business metric.

---

# 30. What We Will Not Fake

The validation page can simulate the product experience.

It must NOT simulate traction.

Do not claim:

**5,000 travelers waiting**

unless true.

Do not claim:

**27 matches found**

if they are presented as actual current users.

Do not create fake testimonials.

Do not label sample users as verified real travelers.

Instead say:

**Preview**

**Example match**

**Here's what your results could look like**

We're validating demand—not deceiving visitors.

# 33. First A/B Test

Don't begin with ten experiments.

Start with one.

### Version A

**Meet someone going where you're going.**

### Version B

**Meet singles before your next trip.**

Everything else stays the same.

Our hypothesis is that Version A is more memorable, while Version B may explain the product more literally.

Measure:

Trip search rate.

Find-match click rate.

Early-access conversion.

Do not optimize merely for page views.

---

# 34. Validation Site Success

The website succeeds if it helps us answer:

**Do travelers understand the concept?**

**Will they search an upcoming destination?**

**Will they give us actual travel dates?**

**Will they share enough dating preferences to participate?**

**Do certain destinations begin clustering?**

**Can compatible supply form?**

**Will travelers volunteer to discuss the problem?**

It does NOT need to prove that we can build a beautiful website.
